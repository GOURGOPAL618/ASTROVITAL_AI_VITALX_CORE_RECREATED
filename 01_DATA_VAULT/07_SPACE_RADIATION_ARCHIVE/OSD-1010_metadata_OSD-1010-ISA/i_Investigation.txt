Study Identifier	OSD-1010
Study Title	Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus GG modulates epithelial responses to space-relevant heavy-ion irradiation in a human gut-on-chip
Study Description	Space radiation poses significant risks to human health, particularly to the radiation-sensitive gastrointestinal tract, yet the role of the microbiome in modulating intestinal responses to space-relevant heavy-ion exposure remains poorly understood due to the lack of advanced human-relevant experimental models. Here, we adapted a previously developed gut-on-chip (HuMiX) that recapitulates host-microbiome crosstalk to investigate the specific role of Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus GG (LGG) on the radiation response of normal human intestinal cells (FHs 74 Int) after exposure to 600 MeV per nucleon Si-28 or 1 GeV per nucleon Fe-56 ions. First, results showed that LGG induced transcriptional reprogramming characterized by reduced proliferative signaling and changes in metabolic and epithelial organization pathway, confirming efficient interaction with the FHs 74 Int cells. Irradiation at an absorbed dose of 50 cGy of Si-28 or Fe-56 ions triggered canonical radiation responses, including activation of inflammatory signaling, DNA damage pathways, p53 signaling, and cell-cycle checkpoints, while suppressing developmental and epithelial differentiation programs. Despite shared radiation-response signatures, the two radiation qualities produced distinct transcriptional profiles: Fe-56 preferentially activated inflammatory and lipid metabolic pathways, whereas Si-28 induced interferon signaling, oxidative phosphorylation, and translation-related programs. Importantly, LGG modulated the radiation response with differences in DNA repair- and replication-associated transcriptional programs. Quantification revealed significantly reduced residual gamma-H2AX-53BP1 DNA damage foci in irradiated LGG-exposed cells compared with irradiated cells cultured without LGG. Together, these findings demonstrate the utility of organ-on-chip platforms to investigate human tissue responses to space radiation and reveal a potential microbiome-mediated mechanism of intestinal radioprotection.

Study Protocol Description	"FHs 74 Int cells (CCL-241) were obtained from and authenticated by the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). Hybri-Care Medium (ATCC, 46-X) was supplemented with 1.5 g/L NaHCO3 (Fisher Scientific, BP328) and 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco). After adjusting the pH to 7, the medium was filtered through a 0.22 um polyethersulfone (PES) membrane, and epidermal growth factor (Shenandoah, 100-26AF) was added at a concentration of 30 ng/mL. FHs 74 Int cells were maintained in complete Hybri-Care Medium at 37 degrees C in a 5% CO2 incubator between passages 4 and 10, with the culture medium changed every 48 to 72 h until loading into the HuMiX device.

Prior to introducing cells into the device, the HuMiX devices were connected to a programmable peristaltic pump (Welco, WPM2-P3EA-WP) through pump tubing (PharMed BPT, Welco, 8413.91), and to the media bottle through platinum-cured silicone tubing (Cole-Parmer, EW-96115-12) and 4-way stopcocks (Braun, D500) secured with threads to the HuMiX chip. First, 1 mL of rat type I collagen (Thermo Fisher, A1048301, diluted to a final concentration of 50 ug/mL in 0.02 M acetic acid) was dispensed into the epithelial cell chamber through the stopcocks to generate a coating that facilitates cell adhesion. After 1 h of incubation at 37 degrees C, unbound collagen was pushed out with a syringe, and a 250 ug/mL porcine gastric mucin solution (Sigma-Aldrich, M2378) was injected into the microbial chamber to provide a feeder layer for bacteria. After 1 h of incubation, excess mucin was pushed out with a syringe, and the HuMiX system was placed at 37 degrees C in a 5% CO2 incubator and primed with complete Hybri-Care Medium by perfusing all three chambers overnight at a flow rate of 21 uL/min. After equilibration, 1 mL of FHs 74 Int cells at a concentration of 1 x 10^6 cells/50 uL was injected into the epithelial chamber and incubated overnight with no flow in all three chambers. The next day, flow was started at 67 uL/min in the epithelial and perfusion chambers and at 21 uL/min in the microbial chamber. After 4 days of incubation under these conditions, the flow was stopped, and the devices were aseptically disconnected from the system by closing all stopcocks and prepared for shipment. Devices were secured in holders, and media bottles and pumps were placed in insulated foam containers. After overnight shipment, the devices were received at Brookhaven National Laboratory (BNL), where they were immediately reconnected and placed back at 37 degrees C in a 5% CO2 incubator, with flow rates restored to 67 uL/min in the epithelial and perfusion chambers and 21 uL/min in the microbial chamber, for at least 6 h to allow system equilibration prior to treatment."	All irradiations were performed at the NASA Space Radiation Laboratory (NSRL) at BNL. HuMiX devices were disconnected from the pumps by closing all the stopcocks at inlets and outlets, secured in a custom foam holder oriented perpendicular to the incident beam. HuMiX devices were then irradiated with 50 cGy of 1 GeV/n Fe-56 ions (56Fe) or 600 MeV/n Si-28 ions (28Si) at dose rates of approximately 50 cGy/min. Delivered doses were verified by the NSRL physics/dosimetry team using calibrated NIST-traceable ion chambers positioned both upstream and downstream of the target location. Following irradiation, devices were placed at 37°C in a 5% CO2 incubator for approximately 2 h to allow for decay of any residual activation products. Subsequently, the devices were reconnected to the perfusion platform and maintained under continuous flow for an additional 24 h prior to downstream analyses.	Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus GG (LGG, ATCC BAA-3227) was obtained from and authenticated by ATCC and cultured in aerobic Lactobacilli MRS broth (ATCC medium 416) in a shaking incubator at 37 degrees C and 200 rpm. Before inoculation, bacteria were pelleted and resuspended in complete Hybri-Care Medium and MRS broth (1 to 1) at 50 million cells per mL (OD600 of 0.06). At BNL, after re-equilibration of the HuMiX devices, 1 mL of LGG suspension was inoculated into the top microbial chamber and incubated for 1 hour without flow. Perfusion of the microbial chamber was then restarted at 21 microliters per minute until irradiation the following day. Devices without LGG were handled identically without bacterial inoculation. Tissue culture plate samples were not co-cultured with LGG.	"For FHs 74 Int cells cultured in HuMiX, medium was first removed from the epithelial chamber by continuing perfusion through the chamber inlet at a flow rate of 21 uL/min while the chamber outlet was detached from the system, allowing the supernatant to be collected into a glass vial. The inlet tubing was then raised above the medium level for all chambers, with the pumps running, to remove free medium from the chambers and avoid contamination from the microbial chamber while disassembling the device. The devices were then disconnected completely from the HuMiX system and disassembled by using a scalpel to separate the chambers, which are held together with double-sided adhesive. Membranes from the epithelial cell chamber were collected, cut in half, and placed in cell lysis buffer for total RNA extraction according to the manufacturer's instructions (Amersham RNAspin Mini Kit, 25050071).

For FHs 74 Int cells cultured in T-75 flasks, cells were harvested with 0.25% trypsin and neutralized with complete medium. The cell suspension was pelleted at 130 x g for 7 min, and RNA was extracted using the Amersham RNAspin Mini Kit (25050071) according to the manufacturer's instructions.

RNA was stored at -80 degrees C until use. Sample H-Fe-L-2 was excluded from downstream analysis due to substantially reduced read mapping performance (lower total and uniquely mapped reads and markedly decreased mapping rates), an abnormal GC content distribution enriched for extremely low-GC reads (0-2%), and outlier clustering in principal component analysis."	RNA was extracted using the Amersham RNAspin Mini Kit (25050071) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Briefly, cell pellets were lysed in Lysis Solution supplemented with 1% β-mercaptoethanol. The cell lysates were then transferred to an RNAspin Mini Filter, centrifuged at 11,000 × g for 1 min, collected, and mixed with one volume of 70% ethanol. After vortexing, the lysate was loaded onto an RNAspin Mini Column and centrifuged at 8,000 × g for 30 s. The flow-through was discarded, and the column was washed with Desalting Buffer before being centrifuged at 11,000 × g for 1 min. DNase was then applied directly to the column and incubated for 15 min at room temperature before the addition of the first Wash Buffer. The column was subsequently centrifuged at 11,000 × g for 1 min, followed by two additional wash steps. Finally, RNA was eluted with RNase-free water and stored at −80 °C until use. RNA quality and quantification were performed by Azenta Life Sciences using Nanodrop 2000 and TapeStation.	Library preparation and RNA sequencing were performed by Azenta Life Sciences. RNA-seq libraries were prepared using a poly(A)-selection workflow. Briefly, polyadenylated mRNA was enriched from total RNA, fragmented, and reverse-transcribed using random primers to generate first-strand cDNA. Second-strand cDNA synthesis was subsequently performed to produce double-stranded cDNA. The resulting cDNA fragments underwent end repair, 5′ phosphorylation, and 3′ dA-tailing prior to adapter ligation. Adapter-ligated libraries were then PCR-amplified and sequenced on an Illumina platform.	RNA-seq was performed by Azenta Life Sciences using an RNA-seq package including polyA selection and sequencing using an Illumina HiSeq for 150 bp paired-end reads.	The sequence reads were trimmed using Trimmomatic v.0.36 to eliminate adapter sequences and nucleotides with poor quality. The trimmed reads were mapped to the Homo sapiens GRCh38 reference genome available on ENSEMBL using the STAR aligner v.2.5.2b. Unique gene hit counts were calculated by using featureCounts from the Subread package v.1.5.2.	Fastq format was checked with fastq_utils v0.25.1. Quality assessment of reads was performed with FastQC v0.11.9 and reports were combined with MultiQC v1.12. rRNA percentages of reads were estimated with the 'hts_SeqScreener' program of htstream v1.4.1 screened against a fasta file of H-sapiens rRNA sequences retrieved from NCBI on 08-Jan-2026.
